Why does reconstruction fail to predict generative performance?

In our paper, we show that reconstruction and generation evaluate the decoder on different latent distributions.Generation-aware reconstruction aligns the two pathways and restores reconstruction as a predictor of generative quality.

01 / Motivation

Two pathways.
Two latent distributions.

Standard reconstruction maps an image to an encoder latent ze ∼ Pe, then asks the decoder to recover the input. Generation starts from noise and produces a generative latent zg ∼ Pg. The same decoder receives inputs from different distributions.

rFID measures decoder behavior under Pe, while gFID depends on behavior under Pg. The evaluation mismatch explains why strong reconstruction can coexist with weak generation.

The comparison isolates the missing variable in standard reconstruction: the latent distribution supplied to the decoder. GAR changes that distribution without changing the paired evaluation protocol.

02 / Method

Reconstruction along
the generative trajectory

GAR perturbs encoder latents, denoises them with the frozen generative model, and evaluates the decoder on the resulting intermediate generative latents.

The noise level ηt traces a continuous path from reconstruction to generation. At ηt = 0, GAR recovers standard reconstruction; at ηt = 1.0, it starts from pure noise and becomes generation.

03 / Distributional gap

A measurable path from Pe to Pg

Latent-FD quantifies the high-dimensional gap between latent distributions. As ηt increases, Pgt moves away from Pe and aligns with Pg.

The two distances trace opposite directions across the same GAR trajectory.

04 / Evaluation

Does GAR-FID
predict generation?

05 / Structural fidelity

Where does structure break along the path?

GAR reconstructions expose this transition at fixed noise levels from standard reconstruction to pure generation.

The progression separates semantic plausibility from structural fidelity. It also motivates training the decoder on intermediate latents where paired supervision remains meaningful.

06 / Decoder adaptation

Can alignment improve
the generator?

Decoder adaptation freezes the encoder and generative model, then fine-tunes only the decoder on intermediate generative latents. It adds no inference cost.

07 / Takeaways
  1. Standard reconstruction evaluates Pe, while generation depends on decoder behavior under Pg.
  2. GAR evaluates intermediate distributions Pgt along the model’s generative trajectory.
  3. GAR-FID reaches near-perfect correlation with gFID across tokenizers and generator scales.
  4. Decoder adaptation improves gFID at every tested iMF scale without increasing inference cost.
